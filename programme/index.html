Layer 02 · Reasoning Computation

RC programme

We lack a sufficiently direct, causally tested account of how LLMs represent, transform, maintain and recover from intermediate computational states during multi-step reasoning.

Directions are research territory, not promised papers. Nothing here is a completed experiment unless cited as published work.
Motivation

The questions RC asks

Q1

How does a model represent intermediate computational states?

Q2

How does it update and manipulate those states across steps?

Q3

How does it decide which intermediate information to use?

Q4

When an intermediate computation is wrong, why does the model sometimes detect and correct it, and at other times propagate it?

Q5

Can understanding these mechanisms help build more reliable models, through training, mechanisms or architecture?

Why it matters

Three stakes

Scientific

Reasoning should be understood as computation with internal mechanisms, not only as an input-output capability. Behaviours that look the same from outside can imply very different internal processes.

Technical

Small computational mistakes compound into large failures. Knowing which mechanisms carry errors forward points to targeted fixes.

Long-term

The aim is not an external checker wrapped around model outputs. It is to understand the computation well enough to inform training methods, model mechanisms and architecture.

Evidence chain

From behaviour to mechanism

01

Construct controlled tasks with exact answers, and transformations that keep the problem while changing its surface or computational demands.

02

Measure behavioural stability, failure profiles and responses to controlled state changes.

03

Inspect activations to see whether candidate values, bindings, rules and intermediate results are represented.

04

Use causal interventions to test whether those representations take part in computation rather than merely correlating with answers.

05

Reconstruct candidate computational pathways and algorithms.

06

Test whether mechanism-targeted interventions improve reliability without damaging correct behaviour, and whether effects transfer.

Separation

RC is not RvC or RR

RvC

Retrieval versus Computation

Do models preserve answers and procedures under controlled transformations; what behavioural evidence separates procedural execution from familiarity?

Separate project and repository. Not reused as Paper 1 infrastructure without a suitability check.

RR

Reasoning Reliability

Can we measure failures, predict them, localize internal causes, intervene, verify improvements and test transfer?

Separate programme and repository. No completed Q1 experiments to inherit.

RC

Reasoning Computation

How do LLMs internally represent, transform, update and use computational state; why do computations fail; can this knowledge improve models?

Paper 1 is RC's first study. Own question, experiments and repository.

Sibling site: Retrieval vs Computation.

Claim discipline

Evidence ladder

Example

Editing a written value in the trace changes the next step

Typical evidence

Token edits with teacher-forced replay or resampled continuation

Does not establish

Any internal representation or route

Direction 1's written-value interventions sit on the behavioural rung. Only pre-write activation interventions reach causal involvement. Paper 1 does not claim the computational-role rung.

Interpretability toolkit

Five layers

1. Readout

Make candidate internal information readable

Logit lens, tuned lens; J++ Lens, Chain Lens, MicroLens

Readable does not mean used

2. Representational basis

How information is encoded

Neurons, linear probes, ICA, DAS, SAEs

Compare bases; none is ground truth

3. Causal influence

Change activations and measure downstream effects

Activation patching, path patching, EAP, ablation, DAS

A patch shows sensitivity, not an independent pathway

4. Computational organization

Which components and paths are responsible

ACDC, EAP, SAE circuits, transcoders, causal scrubbing

Only after a reproducible causal effect

5. Improvement

Targeted changes that improve reliability

Steering, ablation, mechanistic shaping
