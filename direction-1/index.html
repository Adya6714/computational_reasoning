Layer 03 · Paper 1 · Research Direction 1

Error propagation

After the model itself writes a wrong intermediate value, what does the next dependent step actually use?

Research question. How do incorrect intermediate values causally influence subsequent computation in language models, and what determines whether those errors are propagated or corrected?
Problem

Same outputs, different computations

When a model generates an incorrect intermediate value and later consumes it, several behaviours are possible. They can produce similar outputs while implying very different internal computations — and they are not mutually exclusive.

What is still insufficiently characterized

Causal use of incorrect intermediate values

Kudo et al.'s key comparison is on correct runs. Wrong values remain under-characterized.

Whether correctness gates use

Correctness is decodable (Yuan et al.), but its causal effect on downstream use of a written value is not established.

Copying versus recomputation after an error

Copying and reconstruction were shown in different settings, not on model-generated wrong values.

Detection versus functional recovery

An error signal is not evidence the model uses it to correct course.

Thinking-mode resilience

Sinha et al.'s stability result is confounded by stripped history. Supporting question only.

Competing explanations

Flip a card — claim vs Paper 1 prediction

These are not mutually exclusive. The objective is to identify which information causally affects downstream computation under stated conditions.

Contribution boundary

What Paper 1 can claim — if supported

A causal profile of how later reasoning depends on a model's own naturally generated incorrect intermediate values, through the written value, source information, or internal state formed before writing — and a test of whether that profile predicts which errors propagate or are corrected during free-running reasoning.

Primary

Causal dependence profile on natural errors plus the replay-to-free-running predictive test.

Methodological

W/S/P intervention design with explicit validation and controls.

Secondary

Same-weights thinking comparison; retained-versus-stripped history; slip versus systematic errors.

Infrastructure

Generators, alignment, intervention tooling, cost estimation — not a scientific contribution.

How it will be tested

Design summary

Data

Controlled multi-step tasks with exact solvers, derived from established benchmarks after an audit. Explicit intermediate values and dependency graphs.

Model

One open-weight checkpoint for the pilot (candidate: Qwen3-8B), with same-weights thinking-on/off comparisons later.

Events

The model's own naturally generated wrong intermediate values, kept in full with every exclusion logged.

Two measurements

Teacher-forced replay (next-step response under fixed context) and free-running continuation (what the model actually does next).

Prediction test

Do W/S/P dependence measures predict propagation or recovery beyond a behavioural baseline?

First milestone

A 2 to 10 event pilot that validates the pipeline and looks for an early scientific signal against pre-written expectations.
